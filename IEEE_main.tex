\begin{abstract}
Multipartite entangled states enable quantum-network protocols to use redundancy even when their goal is to distribute a Bell pair. All-photonic repeaters exemplify this approach, motivating languages that capture the manipulation of intermediate multipartite resources. We outline a vision for an algebraic language for specifying and reasoning about these protocols, inspired by BellKAT and its tools for Bell-pair distribution. We identify three connected design challenges: representing interchangeable quantum resources, specifying the effects of primitive operations, and composing these operations into protocols. As an initial design, we consider graph states modulo local Clifford operations and qubit relabelings within each node, together with actions specifying their input and output resources. Our aim is to support compositional verification and analysis of multipartite entanglement distribution.
\end{abstract}
