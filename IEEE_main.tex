\begin{abstract}
Quantum-network protocols increasingly use multipartite entangled states, including photonic graph states, to distribute entanglement across network nodes. Specifying such protocols requires a representation of their quantum resources that is tractable while retaining the distinctions relevant to protocol actions. We propose an algebraic language whose network states are multisets of graph-state classes under local complementation and permutations of qubits within a node. Probabilistic actions describe state creation, transmission, measurement, and fusion, and compose across synchronous rounds. A fusion example shows that an operation on equivalent input graph states can yield inequivalent outputs; our actions therefore specify their output classes explicitly. This provides a starting point for reasoning about multipartite entanglement-distribution protocols beyond Bell-pair resources.
\end{abstract}
