We outline an initial design around the three questions above. It models pure stabilizer resources and probabilistic, heralded outcomes; a full treatment of noise and loss recovery remains part of the research agenda.

\subsection{State}
\label{sec:state}

A resource graph includes a location map $\ell:V\to N$ assigning each qubit to a network node. We propose identifying graphs related by local complementations and permutations preserving $\ell$, and write $\lcpClass{G}$ for the resulting \emph{local-complementation-and-permutation (LCP) class}. Node locations remain observable. An implementation may rename qubits within a node, but transmission changes $\ell$ and is an explicit action. We assume interchangeable local qubits, available single-qubit Clifford gates, and known measurement corrections; local adaptation contributes no time or failure probability in this initial model. Photonic modes can serve as the local qubits, without long-lived memories.

A network state $s\in\Ms{\LcpSet}$ is a multiset of independent resource copies, where $\LcpSet$ contains the LCP classes of connected graph states, including single qubits. Copies occupy disjoint physical qubits; their representative labels do not reserve hardware addresses. To avoid representing the same product state both as one resource and as several, we split disconnected graphs into connected components:
\[
\resources{G}\triangleq
\ms{\lcpClass{G_1},\ldots,\lcpClass{G_k}}.
\]
Here $G_1,\ldots,G_k$ are the components of $G$, each retaining its location map. This convention also applies when a measurement splits a resource.

\subsection{Actions}
\label{sec:actions}

A candidate action $a=\act{r}{\sum_i p_i\cdot o_i}$ requires resources $r$ and specifies a finite distribution over output multisets $o_i$, with $p_i\geq0$ and $\sum_i p_i=1$. Its proposed effect is
\[
s\xrightarrow{a,p_i}(s\setminus r)\uplus o_i
\quad\text{if }r\subseteq_{\mathrm m}s;
\qquad
s\xrightarrow{a,1}s
\quad\text{otherwise}.
\]
The symbols $\subseteq_{\mathrm m}$, $\setminus$, and $\uplus$ denote multiset inclusion, subtraction, and addition. Unused resources remain available. Failure is an outcome that may consume inputs, whereas an action with unavailable inputs leaves the network unchanged.

For example, let $r=\resources{G_a}=\resources{G_b}$ for the figure's inputs. Two possible specifications are
\[
\begin{aligned}
a&=\act{r}{\bigl(p_a\cdot\resources{G_{a'}}+(1-p_a)\cdot\emptyset\bigr)},\\
b&=\act{r}{\bigl(p_b\cdot\resources{G_{b'}}+(1-p_b)\cdot\emptyset\bigr)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
An implementation of $a$ prepares representative $G_a$ before fusion; one of $b$ prepares $G_b$. The probabilities refer to the respective implementations, conditioned on their inputs. Here failure discards all resources involved in the attempt, an explicit simplifying policy. In general a failed fusion can leave useful resources, represented by a nonempty output.

Creation requires no inputs; transmission changes qubit locations; and Pauli measurement removes measured qubits and returns the corrected remaining components. Measurement branches may be combined when they yield the same resource classes after known corrections. A branch that leaves an unmodeled mixed state requires discarding the affected resource or a richer state domain. Discarding after loss is a policy, not a claim that all surviving entanglement is unusable.

Specifying outputs is a contract, not a physical realizability test. Each admitted action must have an implementation that realizes its stated distribution from any representative of its required classes, using the allowed local adaptation. This requirement excludes arbitrary transformations and makes representative independence a concrete proof obligation.

\subsection{Composition of Actions}
\label{sec:composition}

Following PBKAT~\cite{buckley2025pbkat}, we envisage synchronous rounds: sequential composition $e;f$ advances between rounds, while parallel composition $e\parallel f$ schedules actions within a round. Parallel actions must consume disjoint resource copies; competition for insufficient resources introduces nondeterminism. Ordered composition $e\circ f$ gives $e$ first access to available resources and $f$ access to the remaining inputs in that round. Outputs become available in the next round. Guards test the abstract network state, and conditionals and while loops express decisions and retries. Actions must be chosen at a granularity compatible with the modeled round duration.

For a small example, $c_A,c_B$ prepare three-qubit paths $A1\edge A2\edge A3$ and $B1\edge B2\edge B3$. Transmissions $t_A,t_B$ move $A3$ and $B1$ to $T$, where they are labeled $T1,T2$. Fusion $f=\fuse{T1,T2}$ then produces the distributed path $H=A1\edge A2\edge B2\edge B3$ on success. One attempt is
\[
t=(c_A\parallel c_B);(t_A\parallel t_B);f.
\]
Assume ideal preparation and transmission, fusion success probability $q$, and discarding both input resources on failure. Starting empty, round one creates two local paths; round two changes their locations; round three produces $\ms{[H]}$ with probability $q$ or $\emptyset$ otherwise. A loop repeating $t$ while $[H]$ is absent expresses retry. Under independent identical attempts, success within $n$ attempts has probability $1-(1-q)^n$, illustrating a question the envisioned analysis should answer.

The next step is to establish that the primitive contracts compose and to determine which BellKAT/PBKAT laws remain sound. In particular, equivalence of complete protocols must account for both outcome probabilities and resource competition.
