% Def. QNs
\emph{\Acp{qn}} are built for the goal of distributing entanglement across separate interconnected nodes.
%
% Foundational QNs (BPs key resource)
Foundational works~\cite{zhu2025remote} focus on the distribution of \emph{\acp{bp}}, i.e., a pair of \ale{maximally} entangled qubits. \Acp{bp} are a key resource in \acp{qn} as they are used in a wide range of applications, such as quantum key distribution~\cite{} and distributed quantum computing~\cite{caleffi2024dqcsurvey}.\alenote{more/change applications, e.g. change/cut distrib. QC}
%
\Ac{bp} distribution is not a trivial task, as it has to deal with channel losses that grow exponentially with the distance of the nodes and qubit decoherence. Quantum repeaters have been devised to address these losses, but they typically require quantum memories, which are challenging experimental hardware~\cite{}.

% Modern QNs (e.g. all photonic) use stabilizer (graph?) states
Modern \acp{qn}~\cite{munro2012,azuma2015_all-photonic,naphan2026bridging,niu2023_all-photonic} leverage multipartite entangled states, namely \emph{stabilizer states}, to introduce redundancy in the distribution protocol and facilitate the distribution of \acp{bp} without requiring quantum memories.
%
Multipartite entanglement is a key resource for applications of quantum computation and communication,
such as multipartite quantum key distribution~\cite{epping2017multipartiteqkd,murta2020cka}, 
blind quantum computing~\cite{} 
and distributed quantum computing~\cite{caleffi2024dqcsurvey}.\llc{repeated}
Furthermore, stabilizer states are the core of \emph{quantum error correction}, which provides a path towards fault-tolerant quantum technologies by encoding information redundantly across larger quantum systems~\cite{nielsen2010quantum}.
%
For instance, all-photonic entanglement distribution protocols~\cite{azuma2015_all-photonic,naphan2026bridging} produce intermediate stabilizer states and \emph{fuse}~\cite{loebl2025bellfusion} them into larger ones~\cite{fischer2020distributinggraph}.
In order to specify and analyze these protocols, one % \ale{a user?} 
must be able to efficiently describe stabilizer states and operations on them.

Previous work~\cite{buckley2024bellkat,buckley2025pbkat,lacorte2026pbkatQCNC} has formalized \ac{bp} distribution protocols in arbitrary \acp{qn} by developing an algebraic language and a software tool to respectively define and analyze \ac{bp} distribution protocols. In \cref{sec:problem} we delve into what was done and what is needed to analyze stabilizer state distribution protocols instead.
